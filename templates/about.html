Grover's Algorithm is an important algorithm in quantum computing. It works by tuning the amplitudes of the quantum states to increase the probability of measuring the desired output through a process called amplitude amplification, which involves applying a series of quantum gates to the original state, which is an even superposition of the possible outcomes. The algorithm is particularly useful for searching unsorted databases, as it can find the desired item in O(√N) time, which is an exponential speedup compared to the classical time complexity of O(N).

The Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) is a hybrid algorithm that finds approximate solutions to combinatorial optimization problems. It works by encoding the problem into a quantum circuit and then using a classical optimizer to adjust the parameters of the circuit to minimize the cost function, a Hamiltonian. The algorithm consists of two main steps; first, the quantum circuit is prepared with a set of parameters, and then the cost function is evaluated by measuring the output of the circuit. The classical optimizer then updates the parameters based on the results of the measurements, and this process is repeated until convergence. QAOA is particularly useful for solving NP-hard problems, which are problems that cannot be solved polynomial time.
